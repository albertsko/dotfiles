## Topic 48: The Essence of Agility

Agility describes how developers respond to change through continuous feedback rather than following a static process. It is an adjective that defines a working style, not a noun or product that teams can purchase. Developers achieve agility by taking small steps, evaluating outcomes, and designing software to remain easy to change.

### The Pragmatic Approach

Pragmatic developers handle uncertainty by gathering and acting on feedback at every level of development. To work in an agile way:

1. Determine the current situation.
2. Take the smallest meaningful step toward the goal.
3. Evaluate the result and fix any broken elements.

Apply this cycle recursively to variable naming, architectural choices, and team workflows. Pair this feedback loop with good software design so that code remains easy to modify when adjustments are necessary.

### Common Mistakes

- Treating agility as a fixed process, rigid methodology, or off-the-shelf product.
- Prioritizing processes, tools, documentation, and contracts over human interactions, working software, and customer collaboration.
- Adding management bureaucracy, specialized roles, and rigid reporting mechanisms in the name of agile practices.
- Failing to continuously review and adapt team practices based on real-world feedback.
