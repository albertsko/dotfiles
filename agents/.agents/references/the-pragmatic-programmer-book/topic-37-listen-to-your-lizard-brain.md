## Topic 37: Listen to Your Lizard Brain

Developers must recognize and act on subconscious instincts when facing friction or hesitation in software development. Experience creates tacit knowledge that surfaces as gut feelings, uncomfortable friction, or reluctance rather than explicit verbal thoughts. When starting a task feels unnerving or writing code feels unusually difficult, the nonconscious brain signals an underlying flaw in design, structure, or assumptions.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Stop coding when facing persistent friction or hesitation.
- Take a break away from the keyboard to let subconscious ideas percolate.
- Externalize the problem by sketching diagrams or explaining the design to a colleague or a rubber duck.
- Build a throwaway prototype to explore ideas without the pressure of writing production code.
- Write a single comment stating the goal of the prototype, attach a visual reminder that the code is temporary, and discard the prototype once clarity returns.
- Analyze instincts regarding legacy code, system designs, or requirements to spot flaws before implementation.

### Common Mistakes

- Ignoring feelings of hesitation and forcing code forward despite increasing complexity and friction.
- Dismissing instincts as irrational fear rather than signals grounded in past experience.
- Attempting to force solutions through continuous coding instead of stepping away.
- Retaining temporary prototype code in production instead of discarding the prototype and writing fresh code.
- Slogging through strange legacy code without analyzing the design decisions that drove its structure.
