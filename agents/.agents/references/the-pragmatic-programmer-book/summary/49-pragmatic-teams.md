## 49. Pragmatic Teams

Pragmatic teams are small, stable groups of roughly a dozen or fewer people whose members know, trust, and depend on one another. They collectively own built-in quality, monitor changes to scope and constraints, and schedule maintenance, process improvement, technology experiments, and learning instead of waiting for spare time. They communicate quickly and effortlessly within the team to avoid duplicated work while presenting a clear, consistent identity to the organization. They combine all the skills needed to deliver small end-to-end increments without gates or handoffs, automate repeatable work, and give individuals enough structure to deliver value without suppressing their autonomy.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Keep teams small and stable so members can build trust and depend on one another.
- Make every member responsible for built-in quality instead of delegating it to a quality officer, and fix small defects before they accumulate.
- Monitor scope, deadlines, requirements, features, and operating environments so gradual changes remain visible. Track new requirements with metrics, and prefer a burnup chart when added features move the goalposts. Do not reject changes automatically; make them visible so the team can respond deliberately.
- Schedule legacy maintenance, process refinement, and team-wide learning as real work.
- Vet candidate technologies with scheduled prototypes and analysis instead of adopting them because they are popular.
- Make internal questions, progress, problems, and discoveries easy to share immediately.
- Encourage lively internal debate, then speak externally with one voice through well-prepared meetings and clear, accurate, consistent documents. Give the project a memorable name and simple logo, and use them consistently to build a recognizable team identity.
- Put every skill required for end-to-end delivery on the team, and use small working increments to expose and improve coordination.
- Keep developers close enough to actual users to understand the context of their work and make informed decisions.
- Automate formatting, testing, builds, and deployment so repeated work stays consistent and reliable. Develop the tool-building skills needed to create and deploy that automation.
- Provide enough structure to support delivery, then let each person contribute in their own way.
