## 2. The Cat Ate My Source Code

Pragmatic programmers take responsibility for their work, learning, careers, commitments, and mistakes. Mutual trust lets teammates rely on one another, speak candidly, and collaborate creatively. Responsibility is an active commitment to ensure an outcome is handled correctly, even without direct control over every factor. Before accepting responsibility, assess risks outside your control and use your values and judgment to decline situations that are impossible, unacceptably risky, or ethically questionable. After accepting it, expect accountability, admit ignorance or errors honestly, and prepare contingencies. When problems arise, replace blame and excuses with practical options to recover, prevent recurrence, obtain resources, learn what is missing, or ask for help.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Build mutual trust by being reliable, relying on teammates, and speaking candidly.
- Assess risks, feasibility, and ethical implications before accepting responsibility.
- Honor accepted commitments and expect accountability for their outcomes.
- Admit mistakes and gaps in knowledge honestly, then find the missing answers.
- Anticipate failures outside your control and prepare contingency plans.
- Test excuses before voicing them by considering what others will ask and what else you can try.
- Offer concrete options to salvage the situation: remove or refactor code when necessary, prototype to choose an approach, or improve testing and automation to prevent recurrence. Do not blame tools, vendors, managers, or coworkers.
- Ask for the resources, user input, training, or help needed to succeed.
