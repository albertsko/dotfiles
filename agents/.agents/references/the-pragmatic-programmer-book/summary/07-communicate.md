## 7. Communicate!

Effective communication turns ideas, code, and decisions into shared understanding and influence by conveying the intended meaning to people with different needs, priorities, and experience. Treat natural language with the same discipline as code: define the message, adapt its timing, style, and presentation to the audience, gather feedback, listen, and respond. Apply the same care to online communication and build documentation into development, keeping it close to the code and using comments to explain purpose, decisions, and trade-offs instead of repeating what the code already shows.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Identify the audience's needs, interests, and capabilities, then explain the benefits in terms that matter to them.
- Decide what you want to say, outline it, and refine it until it conveys the intended message clearly.
- Choose a relevant moment, account for the audience's current priorities, and ask whether it is a good time to talk.
- Match the delivery's formality, length, and technical depth to the audience, and explain when the requested format cannot carry the necessary information.
- Present written material clearly and consistently, and check spelling both automatically and by hand.
- Share early drafts, invite questions, and use responses, facial expressions, and body language to improve the message.
- Listen actively, ask questions, and have people restate the discussion in their own words to create a dialogue and confirm understanding.
- Reply to messages promptly, even when the immediate response only promises a later answer.
- Treat email and social posts as permanent records: proofread them, keep formatting and quotations concise, attribute quoted material, verify recipients, and send nothing you would refuse to say face to face.
- Build documentation into the development process, document modules and exported functions for their users, and record purpose, goals, trade-offs, decisions, and rejected alternatives without duplicating the code's mechanics.
