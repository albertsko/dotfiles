## 47. Working Together

Working together means collaborating with users, developers, testers, and sponsors while writing code, not merely collecting input through meetings, notes, or exhaustive documentation. A system tends to mirror its team's communication structure, so including users in development makes their knowledge visible in the software, while isolated teams tend to produce siloed designs. Pair programming places two developers on one problem with one person typing and the other considering broader concerns, combining perspectives, attention, accountability, and mental capacity; mob programming extends the same live-coding collaboration to a larger, diverse group for difficult problems.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Include users, testers, sponsors, and other relevant experts in live coding sessions.
- Try pair programming for at least two weeks in sessions lasting only a few hours.
- Use a small mob of four or five people to brainstorm ideas or diagnose difficult problems.
- Rotate the typist in a mob every five to ten minutes.
- Start with a simple exercise before applying a new collaboration style to critical production code.
- Focus on building the code, criticize the code rather than the person, and listen to different viewpoints.
- Hold frequent retrospectives and adjust the collaboration process.
- Study the practices, benefits, and pitfalls of each collaboration style before adopting it.
