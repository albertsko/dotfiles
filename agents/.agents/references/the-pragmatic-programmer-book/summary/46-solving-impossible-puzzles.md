## 46. Solving Impossible Puzzles

Problems that seem impossible often stay stuck because people repeat obvious approaches and mistake preconceived notions for absolute constraints. Solve them by finding the true boundary of the problem: identify every real constraint, preserve the absolute ones, challenge the assumed ones, and use the available degrees of freedom instead of dismissing unconventional solutions. When effort becomes unproductive, step away or explain the problem to someone else so nonconscious association and probing questions can reveal simpler formulations, removable edge cases, or a reason not to solve it; prepare for future insights by learning from what works and what fails, and do not panic.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Enumerate every possible avenue, including options that initially sound impractical or foolish.
- Identify each constraint, classify it as absolute or assumed, and demand proof before rejecting a path.
- Rank the most restrictive constraints first, then fit the remaining constraints within them.
- Ask whether the problem must be solved, whether it must be solved in the proposed way, and what benefit the solution provides.
- Remove avoidable edge cases and try a simpler related problem when the full problem remains stuck.
- Stop forcing an unproductive approach; switch tasks, take a walk, sleep on the problem, or explain it to someone who can ask probing questions.
- Record what works and what fails so prior experience can feed future insights.
- Stay calm.
