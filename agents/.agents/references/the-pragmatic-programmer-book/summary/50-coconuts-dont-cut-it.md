## 50. Coconuts Don’t Cut It

Copying the visible artifacts, labels, and rituals of a successful team does not reproduce its results because practices work within specific constraints, skills, organizations, cultures, users, and markets. A methodology should help people work together, so teams must treat methods as sources of useful techniques, combine and adapt those techniques to their own circumstances, and reject rules that add waste or overhead. The real goal is not to follow a fashionable method but to deliver working software when users need it, progressively shortening delivery cycles toward safe, on-demand releases. Achieving that goal requires reliable infrastructure, development in the main version-control trunk, controlled rollout techniques such as feature switches, and a willingness to keep changing the process before habit makes adaptation difficult.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Question why each method, framework, tool, and technique belongs in the workflow.
- Evaluate practices against the team’s actual constraints, expertise, culture, users, and business needs.
- Pilot promising ideas with a small group, keep what works, and discard waste and overhead.
- Combine useful practices from multiple methods instead of treating one method as complete or universal.
- Shorten delivery cycles step by step until the team can release safely whenever users need new capabilities.
- Build reliable delivery infrastructure, work in the main version-control trunk, and use feature switches for selective rollouts.
- Reassess the process regularly so familiarity with one methodology does not block better alternatives.
