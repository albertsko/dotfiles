## 50. Coconuts Don’t Cut It

Copying the visible artifacts, labels, and rituals of a successful team does not reproduce its results because practices work within specific constraints, skills, organizations, cultures, users, and markets. A methodology should help people work together, so teams must treat methods as sources of useful techniques, combine and adapt those techniques to their own circumstances, and reject rules that add waste or overhead. No single method is complete across project management, technical practices, and portfolio or governance needs, and memorized certification rules cannot replace judgment about when to go beyond them. The real goal is not to follow a fashionable method but to deliver working software when users need it, progressively shortening delivery cycles toward safe, on-demand releases. On-demand capability does not require constant releases; deliver when users need a capability and when releasing makes business sense. Achieving that goal requires reliable infrastructure, development in the main version-control trunk, controlled rollout techniques such as feature switches, and a willingness to keep changing the process before habit makes adaptation difficult.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Question why each method, framework, tool, and technique belongs in the workflow.
- Evaluate practices against the team’s actual constraints, expertise, culture, users, and business needs.
- Pilot promising ideas with a small group, keep what works, and discard waste and overhead.
- Use judgment to move beyond memorized or certified rules when the context offers a better option.
- Combine useful practices from multiple methods instead of treating one method as complete or universal.
- For a less experienced team, consider Scrum for project management plus eXtreme Programming (XP) technical practices; for a more disciplined, experienced team, investigate Kanban and Lean at team and governance levels.
- Shorten delivery cycles step by step until the team can release safely whenever users need new capabilities.
- Build reliable delivery infrastructure, work in the main version-control trunk, and use feature switches for selective rollouts.
- Reassess the process regularly so familiarity with one methodology does not block better alternatives.
