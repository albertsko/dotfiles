## 50. Coconuts Don’t Cut It

Copying the visible ceremonies, tools, terminology, or structures of a successful team does not reproduce the conditions that made its practices effective. Select and adapt development practices to your product, users, constraints, skills, organization, and culture, then judge them by whether they improve your ability to deliver working software when users need it. Treat every methodology as a collection of testable practices: pilot changes at small scale, measure the results, keep what works, remove overhead, and progressively shorten the delivery cycle toward safe, on-demand releases.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Define the engineering outcome before choosing a method or tool, such as reducing integration failures, review delays, or release lead time.
- Evaluate each practice against the team’s actual context, including system risk, product needs, team expertise, organization size, and delivery constraints.
- Identify the purpose behind every ceremony or artifact. Remove or change a stand-up, iteration, board, or metric when it does not improve coordination, feedback, or delivery.
- Pilot one practice with a small team or limited scope. Establish a baseline, measure the effect, and keep only the parts that produce a useful improvement.
- Combine complementary practices instead of expecting one methodology to cover planning, implementation, testing, deployment, and governance.
- Shorten delivery cycles incrementally. Move from months to weeks, weeks to days, and days to on demand while preserving reliability.
- Build the technical foundation for safe delivery: automate builds, tests, and deployments; integrate work into the main trunk; and use feature switches to expose incomplete or experimental behavior selectively.
- Optimize for the ability to release when users and the business need a change. Do not confuse on-demand capability with an obligation to deploy continuously.
- Reassess established practices regularly. Remove rules and tools that have become waste, and test better alternatives before habit limits the team’s ability to adapt.
