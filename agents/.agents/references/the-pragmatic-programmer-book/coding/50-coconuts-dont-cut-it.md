## 50. Coconuts Don’t Cut It

Copying the visible ceremonies, tools, terminology, or structures of a successful team does not reproduce the conditions that made its practices effective. Select and adapt development practices to your product, users, constraints, skills, organization, and culture, then judge them by whether they help people work together to deliver working software when users need it. Treat methodologies as sources of testable practices: pilot changes, measure the results, keep what works, discard overhead, and progressively shorten the delivery cycle toward reliable, on-demand delivery.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Define the engineering outcome before choosing a method or tool, such as reducing integration failures, review delays, or release lead time.
- Evaluate each practice against the team’s actual context, including the market, system risk, product and user needs, team expertise, organization size, management, culture, and delivery constraints.
- Identify the purpose behind every ceremony or artifact. Remove or change a stand-up, iteration, board, or metric when it does not improve coordination, feedback, or delivery.
- Pilot one practice with a small team or limited scope. Establish a baseline, measure the effect, and keep only the parts that produce a useful improvement.
- Treat named methods, prescribed rules, and certifications as inputs, not authority. Look beyond their rules for opportunities that better fit the work.
- Combine complementary practices instead of expecting one methodology to cover planning, implementation, testing, deployment, and governance. For example, pair Scrum’s project-management practices with technical practices from Extreme Programming (XP), or test Kanban and Lean practices for team and governance work as the team gains discipline and experience.
- Shorten delivery cycles incrementally. Move from years to months, months to weeks, weeks to days, and days to on demand while preserving reliability.
- Build a rock-solid delivery infrastructure, integrate work in the main trunk instead of branches, and use feature switches to roll out test features selectively.
- Optimize for the ability to release when users and the business need a change. Do not confuse on-demand capability with an obligation to deploy continuously.
- Reassess established practices regularly. Remove rules and tools that have become waste, test better alternatives, and avoid investing so heavily in one methodology that the team becomes blind to other options.
