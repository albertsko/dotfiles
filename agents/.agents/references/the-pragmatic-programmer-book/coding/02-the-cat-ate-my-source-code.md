## 2. The Cat Ate My Source Code

Pragmatic programmers build the reciprocal trust needed for candid, creative collaboration by taking responsibility for their work, professional growth, commitments, mistakes, and knowledge gaps. Responsibility is an active agreement to deliver an outcome even when some dependencies are outside your control. Assess feasibility, ethics, and risk before accepting responsibility; after accepting it, anticipate failures, prepare contingencies, and remain accountable. When work is late, broken, or blocked, investigate and explain the facts honestly, then offer recovery and prevention options or request help instead of blaming tools, vendors, managers, or coworkers.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Evaluate a commitment before accepting it. Identify constraints, dependencies, ethical concerns, and failure modes; decline or renegotiate work when no responsible path exists.
- Surface risks early and create contingencies. For example, prepare an alternative supplier or reduce scope if a vendor may miss a critical deadline.
- Admit mistakes and uncertainty directly. Follow “I don’t know” with a specific action and deadline, such as “I’ll reproduce the issue and report what I find by 3 p.m.” Take responsibility for closing skill gaps by seeking focused learning or asking for help.
- Own the outcome even when external factors contribute. Describe a vendor failure as a cause, then state how you will recover and prevent the same dependency from causing another failure.
- Investigate before escalating bad news. Reproduce the problem, inspect available evidence, try reasonable fixes, and anticipate the questions teammates will ask.
- Present actionable options with costs and tradeoffs. Offer choices such as reducing scope, replacing fragile code, building a prototype, adding tests or automation, requesting another engineer, consulting users, or learning a missing technique.
- Restore trust through follow-through. Complete the agreed recovery work and add safeguards such as tested backups, automated checks, or earlier risk reviews.
