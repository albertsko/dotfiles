## 22. Engineering Daybooks

An engineering daybook is a paper notebook for recording work details such as meeting notes, current tasks, learned information, debugging values, reminders, sketches, and ideas. Writing these details creates a more reliable record than memory, preserves unrelated ideas without disrupting concentration, and prompts reflection that can expose incorrect assumptions or mistakes; even doodling may help sustain focus.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Keep a paper notebook and pen available throughout the workday. Use handwriting rather than a file or wiki because the act of writing can encourage reflection in a way typing does not.
- Record concrete context as you work, including meeting notes, the current task, learned information, debugging values, contact details, reminders about where you put things, sketches, ideas, and doodles.
- Capture useful but unrelated ideas immediately, then return your attention to the current task.
- Write down observed values and relevant context while debugging so you can reconstruct the investigation instead of relying on memory.
- Pause while writing an entry to reflect on the action or result you are recording. Challenge assumptions and correct mistakes that the explanation reveals.
- Label each full notebook with its date range and keep it with previous daybooks as a chronological record of your work.
- Follow the practice consistently for one month, then assess whether it improves recall, focus, and reflection.
