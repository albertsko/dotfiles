## 22. Engineering Daybooks

An engineering daybook is a paper notebook for recording work details such as meeting notes, current tasks, debugging values, reminders, sketches, and ideas. Writing these details creates a more reliable record than memory, preserves unrelated ideas without disrupting concentration, and prompts reflection that can expose incorrect assumptions or mistakes.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Keep a paper notebook and pen available throughout the workday.
- Record concrete context as you work, including meeting notes, the current task, debugging values, contact details, decisions, reminders, sketches, and ideas.
- Capture useful but unrelated ideas immediately, then return your attention to the current task.
- Write down observed values and relevant context while debugging so you can reconstruct the investigation instead of relying on memory.
- Explain what you did and what you expected while writing an entry. Use any mismatch the explanation reveals to challenge assumptions and correct mistakes.
- Label each full notebook with its date range and keep it as a searchable chronological record of your work.
- Follow the practice consistently for one month, then assess whether it improves recall, focus, and reflection.
