## 4. Stone Soup and Boiled Frogs

Large improvements often gain support faster when you first build a small, well-executed slice that demonstrates value and gives others a concrete success to extend. Use incremental delivery to catalyze collaboration, but maintain situational awareness: small scope additions, delays, patches, and deviations can accumulate until the system, schedule, or team is in trouble.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Identify the smallest reasonable increment that demonstrates the intended outcome with the resources and support currently available.
- Develop the increment well, demonstrate the working result, show a glimpse of the intended future, and invite stakeholders to support a specific extension.
- Use growing support to bring contributors together around an outcome they could not produce alone.
- Assess how each increment affects everyone involved. Pursue shared benefit, and keep the direction, costs, and risks visible instead of concealing harmful changes or manipulating consent.
- Review the whole project constantly, including scope, schedule, conformance to specifications, patch accumulation, and team morale.
- Track small changes and evaluate their combined impact. Several locally reasonable features, delays, or patches can create an unreasonable system or maintenance burden together.
- Define thresholds that trigger reassessment, such as repeated deadline movement, specification drift, or patches that repeatedly bypass the design.
- Inspect work beyond your assigned tasks. Check adjacent components, dependencies, stakeholder behavior, and delivery trends for changes that do not belong or no longer support the intended outcome.
