## 4. Stone Soup and Boiled Frogs

Large improvements often gain support faster when you first build a small, well-executed slice that demonstrates value and gives others a concrete success to extend. Use incremental delivery to catalyze collaboration, but maintain situational awareness because small scope additions, delays, patches, and deviations can accumulate until the system, schedule, or team is in trouble.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Identify the smallest safe vertical slice that demonstrates the intended outcome with the resources already available.
- Build the slice well enough to make the idea credible, then demonstrate the working result and invite stakeholders to support a specific next increment.
- Turn growing support into explicit commitments: define each increment's scope, owner, resources, acceptance criteria, and effect on the delivery plan.
- Keep the direction, costs, and risks visible so contributors can make informed choices; never use incremental progress to conceal harmful changes or manipulate consent.
- Review the whole project at regular checkpoints, including scope, schedule, architecture, code quality, operational risk, and team morale.
- Track small changes and evaluate their combined impact. Five locally reasonable patches may create an unreasonable maintenance burden when considered together.
- Define thresholds that trigger reassessment, such as repeated deadline movement, rising defect rates, or patches that repeatedly bypass the design.
- Inspect work beyond your assigned tasks. Check adjacent components, dependencies, stakeholder behavior, and delivery trends for changes that do not belong or no longer support the intended outcome.
