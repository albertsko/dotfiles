## Topic 6: Your Knowledge Portfolio

Technical knowledge and experience decline in value over time as technology and market demands evolve. Programmers must actively manage their knowledge like a financial portfolio to prevent their professional assets from becoming obsolete.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- **Invest regularly**: Build a daily habit of learning new material during set times.
- **Diversify learning**: Acquire skills across technical tools, different programming paradigms, and non-technical soft skills.
- **Manage risk**: Balance foundational technologies with high-risk, high-reward emerging tools.
- **Buy low and sell high**: Study emerging technologies before they become mainstream industry standards.
- **Review and rebalance**: Re-evaluate skills periodically to adapt to industry changes.
- **Set specific goals**: Learn one new language every year, read one technical book each month, read non-technical books, take classes, and attend user groups.
- **Pursue answers**: Investigate unknown concepts immediately when encountered rather than leaving them unanswered.
- **Think critically**: Analyze technical claims by asking the "Five Whys", identifying who benefits, and evaluating the context.

### Common Mistakes

- Letting technical skills expire by failing to learn continuously.
- Relying exclusively on a single technology stack or toolset.
- Focusing only on technical skills while neglecting human-centered soft skills.
- Accepting vendor hype and industry trends without critical evaluation.
- Stopping research when encountering an unfamiliar question or concept.
