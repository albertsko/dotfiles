## Topic 52: Delight Your Users

Software development aims to delight users by solving their underlying business problems rather than simply delivering code or meeting incomplete technical specifications. Users evaluate success based on business outcomes such as customer retention, cost savings, or data quality. Developers must look beyond technical requirements to understand and deliver the business value clients expect.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Ask stakeholders: "How will you know that we have all been successful a month or a year after this project is done?"
- Ensure every team member understands the business expectations and uses them to guide implementation decisions.
- Analyze user requirements critically, recognizing that stated requirements are often amateur implementation plans dressed up as specifications.
- Suggest changes to requirements whenever an alternative approach moves the project closer to the underlying business objective.
- Leverage broad exposure across the organization to connect different business areas and solve core problems.

### Common Mistakes

- Treating working code delivery as the primary goal instead of solving business problems.
- Assuming user expectations are fully captured in initial requirement specifications.
- Following stated requirements blindly without evaluating whether they achieve the desired business value.
- Failing to surface how stakeholders will measure project success after deployment.
- Focusing on mining user data or meeting technical metrics instead of filling users with delight.
