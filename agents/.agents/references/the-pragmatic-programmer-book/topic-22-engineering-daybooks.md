## Topic 22: Engineering Daybooks

An engineering daybook is a physical paper journal used to record daily technical work, debugging values, meeting notes, idea sketches, and reminders. Keeping a written daybook improves recall, preserves focus, and enhances reflection during software development.

### The Pragmatic Approach

- Use a physical paper notebook instead of a digital file or wiki because handwriting stimulates cognitive reflection differently than typing.
- Record variable values while debugging, meeting notes, contact details, and temporary reminders to build a reliable record.
- Jot down off-topic or spontaneous ideas immediately to keep the mind focused on the task at hand.
- Use the act of writing as a rubber duck debugging tool to pause, switch gears, and evaluate technical assumptions.
- Review past daybooks periodically to track progress and reflect on historical project details.

### Common Mistakes

- Relying on memory instead of writing down specific technical details and debugging values.
- Using digital note applications or wikis rather than physical paper for daily engineering logs.
- Letting distracting ideas disrupt ongoing work instead of capturing them in a daybook for later review.
- Skipping written reflection during complex problem-solving, missing opportunities to catch flawed logic early.
